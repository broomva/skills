Keel · design standards

An instrument, not a dashboard. The form follows the thesis.

Keel argues that a check is only a check if its signal comes from outside the write boundary. A design language for that argument has one job: never let the artifact make a claim it cannot show the evidence for.

Lineage

The structure of this system is adapted from the Oasis Design System: layered tokens behind a single entry point, self-contained HTML specimen cards per foundation, a per-component usage brief written for an agent rather than a human, a machine-checkable adherence config, and a readme that spends its first third on voice before it names a single color.

The look is deliberately inverted. Oasis is a warm, light, photoreal desert — cream paper, soft shadows, generous radii, a blue orb, a slow calm wobble. It is selling a feeling of work moving forward without friction. Keel is a forensic instrument that tells you your pipeline is green and nothing has been verified. Borrowing that warmth would flatter the reader at exactly the moment the artifact needs to not flatter them.

So: Oasis's rigor, Keel's register. Where a rule below reverses one of theirs — hairlines instead of soft shadows, small radii instead of generous ones, 90ms instead of a living shimmer — the reversal is the point, not an oversight.

Seven principles

01

The instrument reads. It does not reassure.

No status theater. Nothing on a Keel surface congratulates anyone. The palette's green is a classification, never a decoration — see rule 03.

02

Evidence sits adjacent to verdict.

Every class carries its writeBoundary.argument in the same block. A verdict without its causal path is precisely the unaccountable green check this tool exists to name, and shipping one would be the artifact committing the error it reports.

03

The accent is the narrator; the verdict hues are the data.

One accent — --k-accent — for links, the emphasized clause, Keel's own annotations. Four verdict hues for the four classes in the frozen schema. The sets never cross. A green button on a Keel surface is a bug, because it teaches the reader that green means good, and then a green node reads as approval rather than as a classification.

04

The denominator stays on screen.

A ratio never renders without its counts, and not_a_check prints beside the fraction rather than inside it. It is the one shoppable class: misfiling a real check there shrinks the denominator and inflates the score. Its chip is dashed and its swatch is the least attractive in the file, so it never looks like somewhere you want nodes to land.

05

Uncertainty is loud, not gray.

unknown is amber and fails closed. It is not "pending", not "needs review", not muted chrome. It is a traced failure to find the fork point, and it counts against the score exactly like self_referential.

06

Mono is quoted from the world. Sans is Keel talking.

The single most useful rule in the system. Node names, paths, commands, class names, exit codes, hashes — mono, because Keel did not write them. Prose, headings, arguments — sans, because Keel did. Provenance becomes legible before a word is read, which is the same distinction the product is about.

07

Motion is measurement, not delight.

Under 200ms, decelerating, no overshoot. Motion preserves object continuity across a state change; it never draws attention to one. Instruments settle.

Voice

Flat, indicative, unhedged. Keel reports what produced a signal and whether the actor can write to it. It does not editorialize, and it never congratulates.

Casing and naming

  • Sentence case everywhere. Uppercase is reserved for .k-eyebrow section labels and the wordmark.
  • Class names are always the schema spelling, in mono: self_referential, never Self-Referential. It is an identifier from the data model, not a label to prettify — re-spelling it breaks the reader's ability to grep the JSON they were just shown.

Words that are not available

  • passing / failing / good / healthy applied to a grounding ratio. Keel classifies; it does not grade.
  • well tested. A high ratio says the signals come from outside. It says nothing about whether they are sufficient.
  • looks anchored / probably / seems. If the fork point was not traced, the answer is unknown, and there is a class for that.
  • Any check-mark or cross emoji. The green check is the subject of the critique; the artifact does not get to use one. Gated in CI.

Numbers

Always with a denominator. "30 of 70 edges anchored", not "30 anchored". Tabular figures (--k-table--num, .k-ratio__value) so columns of counts align and a changing digit does not shift the row.

Color

Two families that never mix: a narrator accent and four data hues. Plus canvas and ink. That is the whole palette — see the verdict swatches and canvas & ink cards for values and contrast.

not_a_check is inert slate and gets a dashed border rather than a solid one, because it is the only class that changes what the ratio is computed over. --k-smell is aliased to --k-unknown rather than given its own hue: an anchored verdict below 0.5 confidence is claiming to be closer to unknown than its color suggests, and a fifth hue would imply a fifth class. There is no fifth class.

Type

Two system stacks, three weights, no webfont. The provenance rule (principle 06) is what the two families are for. Weight stops at 600 — on a #07090c canvas, heavier strokes bloom, and the halation costs more legibility than the emphasis buys.

One display size is reserved: --k-fs-ratio is for the grounding ratio and nothing else. Using it elsewhere puts a second number at the same visual weight as the only number the artifact is about.

Surface

Elevation is a background step plus a hairline. There are no shadow tokens: on a near-black canvas a shadow is a smudge that costs paint and reads as nothing. Radii are small — 3 to 12px. Keel's surfaces are cells, rows, and records, the vocabulary of a ledger, and generous rounding reads as consumer-soft.

Graph marks are squares. A circle in a verification UI reads as a status dot, and the status dot is the thing being audited.

Motion

--k-dur-fast 90ms for state feedback, --k-dur-base 160ms for anything that moves, --k-ease decelerating with no overshoot. Nothing animates on load. prefers-reduced-motion removes all of it, which costs nothing because none of it is load-bearing.

Components

The component contract is a class name plus a documented markup shape — there is no runtime and no framework to hang an API on. The per-component brief lives in skills/keel/design/README.md, written for the agent that will emit the markup. Specimen cards for the two load-bearing components are on the system home.

The mark, and the exception it carries

The mark is a faceted keel blade cutting down through a lit waterline — the pale part above the surface, the luminous part below doing the structural work where you cannot see it. It is a rendered emblem: glow, gradient, faceted shading. Every one of those is forbidden by the surface rules above.

That exception is deliberate and it is bounded to the emblem. The interface is not a rendered object, and nothing in the product chrome may borrow the treatment. The moment a card or a button grows a glow, the exception has leaked and the austerity the rest of the system depends on is gone. Usage rules live on the system home; the asset contract is in skills/keel/design/README.md.

It also costs coverage. While the marks were SVG the gate compared their inks to tokens.css byte for byte. A raster emblem cannot be checked that way, so brand-colour agreement is now enforced by review. Stated here because a gate that quietly stops covering something is worse than one that never covered it.

Enforcement

make design-audit is a gate, not a linter you may ignore. It fails on a raw hex or a raw px outside the token file, on a site/ copy that has drifted from canonical, on a data-class the frozen schema does not define, on a ratio rendered without its denominator or its scope note, and on a check-mark emoji.

It is deliberately an executed comparison — a byte diff and a parse of schemas/keel.ts — rather than an assertion about values this repo also writes. A gate of the second kind would be self_referential under Keel's own definition, and shipping one in the repository that defines the term would be indefensible.

Checks 1 and 2 have an escape hatch: put audit-ok: on the line with a reason. Checks 3 through 7 have none. They encode product invariants, not style preferences.

Constraints, stated honestly

  • No webfonts, ever. The report artifact must open from file:// with zero external requests, which rules out every hosted face; a base64 face would add ~80KB to an artifact whose whole argument is that it depends on nothing. Both stacks are system stacks and render slightly differently per platform. Accepted. This is where Keel and Oasis had to part ways on method as well as look — Oasis ships Figtree from a CDN, and Keel cannot.
  • --k-ink-3 is below 4.5:1 on the page canvas. Borders, disabled glyphs, and rules only. Never text.
  • The site/ stylesheets are copies. GitHub Pages deploys that directory standalone, so it cannot reach into skills/. The duplication is real; the byte-diff gate is what makes it safe rather than a convention that decays.
  • Dark-first, with a print inversion. The canvas and ink invert for print while the four verdict hues are darkened rather than swapped, so a printed report and a screen report never disagree about what they show.

Scope. Keel measures the shape of verification, not its quality. A repo can be 100% anchored with terrible tests. Anchoring says the signal comes from outside; it does not say the signal is sufficient. Every artifact that prints a grounding ratio carries this note verbatim.
